Atlassian: Go-to-Market Strategy for Jira Service Management

Boston University Questrom · Spring 2026 · Team of four · Top-scoring team

The challenge: Jira Service Management had a large base of Jira customers who had not adopted it, but Atlassian lacked the enterprise sales reach and partner ecosystem to convert them and compete with ServiceNow.

My role: The whole team worked on every stage together, including primary research, the partner program analysis and the three-program strategy.

  • Ran structured stakeholder interviews with a senior go-to-market leader at Atlassian on partner engagement, deal registration and incentives
  • Compared partner program models at AWS, Salesforce, ServiceNow and IBM to find best practices and failure modes
  • Designed three connected programs: a 90-day Agentic Value Sprint, an AI Data Readiness Wedge, and a Managed Services Accelerator planned for Year 2
  • Specified a 90-day Asia-Pacific pilot with day-90 metrics, including time-to-first-demo and sprint-to-close conversion

Outcome: Recommended launching with two anchor GSI partners in Asia-Pacific and validating the model through the pilot before global rollout.

Learning: The main barrier was distribution, not the product. Enterprise buyers trust advisors, so the strategy had to make partners as motivated as Atlassian.

Rothea: Anti-Aging Skincare Consumer Research

Boston University Questrom · Fall 2026 · Team project

The challenge: Rothea, an anti-aging skincare brand, needed to understand how consumers choose products and how to position its messaging against competitors such as CeraVe, Olay and Neutrogena.

My role: The whole team worked on every stage together: secondary research, consumer interviews, survey design and analysis.

  • Researched the anti-aging market and compared Rothea's ingredients and price per ounce against three competitors
  • Conducted consumer interviews and summarized them into findings on messaging, ingredients and price
  • Drafted a consumer survey mapped to five statistical analyses: chi-square, factor and cluster, regression, and one-sample and paired t-tests

Outcome: Interviews suggested that "anti-aging" language felt too direct, and that consumers respond to ingredient-led proof and "skin resilience" messaging. Final results expected by the end of November 2026.

Learning: Consumers choose by ingredient and visible results rather than by age, so messaging that names the problem works better than messaging about age.

Stratco: Expanding Energy-Rebate Services into Indiana

Boston University Questrom · Fall 2026 · Team project

The challenge: Stratco Property Solutions, an NYC-area consultancy that helps building owners cut energy costs and meet regulations, needed a plan for growth, and Indiana was its top-priority state. Most of its services depend on New York rules that don't apply there, so the question was which services could still win in Indiana.

My role: The whole team worked on every stage together: market research, the Indiana analysis, the target-customer matrix and the presentation.

  • Analyzed Indiana's energy rules, rent regulation, the federal 179D deduction and utility rebate programs to test each Stratco service against local demand
  • Built a three-layer matrix covering state, project type and target customers, with apartments as the top project type
  • Wrote a cited Indiana quick update for the client and reviewed the team's presentation deck

Outcome: Identified which services could still work in Indiana, such as utility bill auditing and rebate administration for large multifamily owners, and who to target for each project type. Final recommendation expected by the end of November 2026.

Learning: Owners in Indiana will pay for voluntary savings, not to avoid fines, so the pitch has to change when the regulations behind a business model do not travel.

Staples: Digital Strategy for Revenue Growth

Boston University Questrom · Fall 2026 · Team project

The challenge: Staples.com had strong fulfillment but a website that made it hard for shoppers to buy and gave brands few places to advertise, which limited both sales and advertising revenue.

My role: The whole team worked on every stage together: comparing the Staples and Nykaa websites, diagnosing the problems, and writing the strategy.

  • Compared the Staples.com website with Nykaa, my former company, to find where shoppers face friction
  • Defined the problems before any solutions: no brand banners or carousels, category pages that are plain product grids, too many steps to buy, and fast delivery that the site does not show
  • Ranked the problems by revenue impact and effort, and recommended a roadmap, success measures and risks

Outcome: Banners and carousels ranked highest for revenue impact, and the team found that delivery speed is not the problem, since the weakness is that the site does not tell shoppers how fast delivery is. Final presentation outcome expected by the end of November 2026.

Learning: Define the problem before proposing solutions. Staples already sells ads through its media network, so the case has to be about specific gaps on the site, not about whether ads exist.
